# Disruption Refunds and Passenger Choice — NS-REF-2026

Effective January 1, 2026. Owner: Ticketing Compliance. This fictional Northstar Air policy describes domestic disruption refunds. It does not replace applicable law or establish another airline's obligations. Rebooking and passenger-care benefits are separate decisions.

## 1. Scope and required information
A missed connection does not automatically create a refund entitlement. Assess the change to the whole journey, passenger choice and transportation already used.

### 1.1 Evidence required
Obtain original and replacement final-destination arrival times, cancellation status, ticket coupon usage and the passenger's response to alternatives. A missed connection alone does not prove a significant change or a decision to decline travel. Missing facts should be identified rather than invented.

## 2. Rebooking or refund
Explain both options when a qualifying disruption occurs. A replacement offer must not be presented as the passenger's only remedy.

### 2.1 No-additional-fare rebooking
NS-CONN-2026 may provide alternative travel for weather or airline-controlled disruptions. An offer, acceptance and completed travel are different states. Accepting an offer does not establish that the passenger actually flew.

### 2.2 Refund instead of alternative travel
A cancellation or significant itinerary change can qualify for a refund when the passenger declines the changed itinerary and does not accept replacement travel, credit or other compensation. Rejecting an eligible rebooking does not forfeit an otherwise qualifying refund. Check unused transportation separately from the entire ticket value.

## 3. Qualifying disruption and passenger decision
The approved cause does not determine whether a cancellation or significant itinerary change qualifies. Weather-related changes can qualify just as airline-controlled changes can.

### 3.1 Cancellation
A canceled covered flight qualifies for assessment when the passenger declines substitute travel. Do not treat an ordinary delay as a cancellation. Verify the carrier's actual flight status.

### 3.2 Domestic significant-change threshold
A replacement scheduled to arrive at the final destination at least three hours later than originally scheduled meets the domestic arrival-change threshold. Departure at least three hours earlier can also qualify. Compare timezone-aware scheduled timestamps from the original ticket and replacement offer.

### 3.3 Passenger choice
Record whether the passenger requested a refund, declined replacement travel, accepted a credit or traveled. An unanswered offer does not prove acceptance or rejection. Verify delivery and expiry before deciding what response occurred.

### 3.4 Replacement flight already used
A passenger who flew the replacement generally cannot obtain a full refund for transportation already used under this disruption remedy. Arrival five hours late does not change that rule. Separate remedies for unused travel, downgrade or optional-service fees may still apply.

### 3.5 Final destination, not first-leg delay
A first flight arriving four hours late does not by itself establish the final-arrival change. A later connection may reduce the difference; a short inbound delay can also cause an overnight arrival change. Without replacement final-arrival information, the significant-change test remains unresolved.

### 3.6 Offer status
CREATED, DELIVERED, VIEWED, ACCEPTED, DECLINED, EXPIRED and FLOWN describe different events. DELIVERED means a notification reached the passenger's channel, not that they accepted travel. An internally generated voucher or offer does not close a refund request.

### 3.7 Partly used tickets
If the passenger flew the first segment but declines qualifying onward travel, assess the unused ticket portion using coupon accounting. Do not promise the entire fare or calculate a dollar amount without ticket-value records. Eligibility and refund amount are separate questions.

### 3.8 Other significant changes
A changed airport, additional connection or qualifying cabin downgrade may matter even when the arrival difference is under three hours. Compare the original and replacement itineraries before deciding. Duration is not the only qualifying criterion.

## 4. Amount and processing
An eligibility decision does not establish that payment has been made. Explain any missing accounting or payment information.

### 4.1 Unused value
An entirely unused qualifying ticket may receive a full refund. For partly used tickets, calculate unused transportation and eligible fees from accounting records. Do not estimate value from distance or delay length.

### 4.2 Payment method and timing
Return eligible refunds to the original payment method. Northstar's processing targets are seven business days for credit-card payments and twenty calendar days for other methods after the verified trigger. Missing payment method or trigger date prevents a promised completion date.

## 5. Examples and exceptions
A passenger who declines a replacement arriving twenty hours late may qualify whether the original disruption was mechanical or weather-related. A passenger who flies that replacement generally cannot obtain a full used-ticket refund under the change remedy. A missed connection alone remains insufficient to decide.

## 6. Escalation
When ticket usage, passenger choice or timestamps conflict, explain what must be verified. Seek compliance review for any conflict with applicable law; never substitute a historical courtesy payment for a governing refund rule.
