# U.S. DOT Airline Service Commitments — Reference Digest

> **Research snapshot:** Retrieved 2026-11-18 from the [U.S. DOT Airline Customer Service Dashboard](https://www.transportation.gov/airconsumer/airline-customer-service-dashboard) and [Cancellation and Delay Dashboard](https://www.transportation.gov/airconsumer/airline-cancellation-delay-dashboard). DOT's customer-service dashboard displayed a last-updated date of 2024-12-10. This is a paraphrased explanatory digest and must not be treated as Northstar Air's filed commitment.

## 1. What the dashboard measures
DOT publishes comparisons of commitments made by listed U.S. airlines when significant cancellations or delays are attributable to circumstances within airline control. The dashboard is useful for interpreting the *type* of promises found in real carrier customer-service plans. It is not a universal statement that every airline must provide a free hotel under every circumstance, nor is it evidence that fictional Northstar Air appears on the public carrier list.

### 1.1 Two different evidence classes
A **federal refund requirement** is derived from applicable regulations. An **airline service commitment** is a promise published in a carrier's own customer-service plan or conditions of carriage. These should be labeled differently by an evidence-based answer. The current Northstar policy is controlled by **NS-CARE-2026**, and the regulatory research digest for refunds is **DOT-REF-SNAPSHOT**. This dashboard digest is contextual background, not the deciding authority for a synthetic Northstar case.

## 2. Categories represented by the dashboard
### 2.1 Rebooking commitments
The dashboard compares whether particular listed carriers commit to rebooking affected passengers on the same airline at no extra cost following significant controllable disruptions, and in some cases on partner airlines. It does not prove that every carrier can find an available seat at the requested time. For a Northstar protected connection, the governing synthetic commitment is **NS-CONN-2026 §2.1**.

### 2.2 Meals
The DOT tables distinguish meal or meal-cash-voucher commitments after qualifying waits, commonly discussed around three hours for controllable disruptions. The precise condition, cause, and carrier must come from the applicable published airline plan. Northstar's training policy specifies its own three-hour rule in **NS-CARE-2026 §3**, which should be cited rather than inferred from unrelated real-carrier columns.

### 2.3 Overnight accommodations
Carrier commitments concerning complimentary hotel rooms and ground transport vary. The comparison makes it evident why "all airlines pay for weather hotels" is a poor answer: the dashboard covers airline-controlled disruptions and carrier-specific published commitments. Eligibility can also depend on residence, available rooms, and approved booking procedures described in the relevant carrier's contract.

### 2.4 Scope of controllability
The dashboard's controllable-disruption framing is distinct from the refund decision that may be triggered by any qualifying cancellation or significant change. A passenger with a weather delay might still be eligible for a qualifying refund while not meeting a carrier's controllable-disruption hotel promise. The two decisions should not be merged in retrieval output.

## 3. Comparative matrix — concepts, not invented carrier commitments
| Benefit category | What the DOT dashboard compares | What FlightResolve should do |
|---|---|---|
| Rebooking on same carrier | Carrier-reported no-extra-charge commitments | Evaluate Northstar connection protection separately |
| Partner-airline rebooking | Agreements vary by carrier | Do not promise unless Northstar authority exists |
| Meal vouchers | Waiting-time and controllability commitments | Check NS-CARE-2026 §3 |

| Hotel ground transport | Additional carrier-specific promise | Do not infer from hotel approval alone |
| Cash refund | Separately governed consumer right | Check DOT-REF-SNAPSHOT and NS-REF-2026 |

## 4. Why this matters for retrieval
An embedding model may retrieve the dashboard because it contains terms such as "hotel," "delay," and "rebook." But high topical similarity should not elevate a third-party carrier's commitment above Northstar's policy for a Northstar passenger. Index each regulatory digest with `authority_level` and `applicability` metadata, and require an airline-specific authoritative source for entitlement conclusions.

### 4.1 Safe evidence behavior
When an answer cites the DOT dashboard, label it "industry comparison" or "carrier-commitment reference" and explicitly distinguish it from a deciding Northstar clause. Do not convert an illustrative dashboard row into a claim about the fictional airline. Archived snapshots should record retrieval date because online tables can change.

### 4.2 Useful historical comparisons
These comparisons may inform the wording and realism of synthetic airline documents, but they are not travel facts, flight inventory, or a substitute for the passenger's booking record. A dashboard cannot establish that a particular traveler missed a connection, incurred an overnight wait, or declined a flight offer.
